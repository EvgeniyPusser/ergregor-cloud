Living House · initial model · version 1.0

A house that senses, redistributes, defends, heals and grows

This is what a client receives when they take this model: not a description of an idea, but a contract. What gets built, what gets measured, what counts as success, under which conditions, and what comes back to the library at the end of the first year.

Seven systems, ordered by how fast they answer — seconds for the nerves, hours for the circulation, years for the bone. Each one is marked with what is already established and what is still an open question. Nothing here is presented as finished that is not.

The body

One generalised dwelling, taken from the published US residential stock rather than designed. Your project replaces its fields and keeps its protocol: that is what makes two houses comparable, and comparability is the only reason experience can pass from one to the other.

One reference house, adapted to every project

The library keeps one generalised dwelling so that results can be compared at all. Your house replaces its fields and keeps its protocol. What is measured, what counts as success and the order of the responses do not change — otherwise two projects would not be comparable and nothing could be inherited between them.

Fixed for everyone

  • Which quantities are measured, and how often

  • Degree-hours and kWh as the outcome

The fields above are the ones the published stock supplies for every dwelling in it; the exact parameter names live in the project's housing-characteristics tables. A generator produces the spread its author assumed. A published stock produces the spread that exists — which is the only kind a client actually lives in.

Seven systems, by how fast they answer

A body meets cold with a ladder of measures, cheap before expensive, each with its own trigger. A building has the same measures available and uses none of them in order. That ordering is the substance of this model.

  1. seconds nerves

    Walls that feel laboratory

    Concrete with conductive addition changes its resistance when it cracks, wets or is loaded, and the reinforcement itself carries the measuring current. The wall becomes its own sensor, with nothing mounted on it that a future cost cut can remove.

    BuiltConductive concrete; existing rebar as the electrode pair; three sections plus one plain control
    MeasuredSection resistance, wall temperature and moisture, strain; crack width by hand as ground truth
    SuccessDays of warning before a visible crack · false alarms per year · drift of the control section

    Laboratory results are strong and consistent. What is not established is how much signal survives several winters once temperature and moisture are removed from it.

  2. hours circulation

    One circulation for the whole house established technology

    Water circuits cast into the concrete already turn the building mass into a radiator and a store. That part is ordinary engineering. What is not ordinary is that the same network which senses the walls decides where the heat goes — not a thermostat in the hall.

    BuiltCircuits per zone, core and periphery declared in advance; a conventional loop kept as the comparison
    MeasuredSupply and return temperature, flow, slab temperature, room temperature — per zone
    SuccessSpread between zones · hours of comfort with the source off · season energy against the conventional loop

    The claim under test is not that this works. It is that connecting it to the sensing network is worth its cost.

  3. hours skin · the ladder

    A house that regulates like skin open question

    Narrow the vessels, raise the hair, burn the reserve, shiver, and only then curl up. Five steps, cheapest first, each with a trigger and a price fixed before the season starts. The trigger is the outdoor temperature and the speed at which it is falling — both available in every house today and used in none.

    BuiltHold the core and let the periphery fall · a closable air gap · a store charged in advance · active heat last · close rooms and heat less volume
    MeasuredOutdoor temperature and its rate of fall; core and periphery separately; charge left in the store; which step is active
    SuccessDegree-hours lost in the core · season energy · what the reserve costs outside the crisis · energy per degree-hour avoided, step by step

    This system exists because of a defect found in the measured work below: the house pays for its reserve all winter and is repaid in three days.

  4. weeks healing

    A wall that closes its own cracks laboratory

    Two routes side by side: channels carrying a healing agent that the crack itself tears open, and mineral deposited into the crack by current through the reinforcement. Plus a plain strip, so that the comparison is honest.

    BuiltThree strips in one wall; cracks induced under controlled load; the same crack reopened until the strip stops recovering
    MeasuredCrack width, permeability, recovered load capacity, agent remaining, charge passed — and the sign of the reinforcement potential, continuously
    SuccessCost per repaired metre · strength recovered · repairs remaining · the ratio against a person with a tube of sealant

    The failure mode of this whole idea is polarity: if the reinforcement ever becomes the anode, the current eats the steel instead of healing the wall. It is interlocked, monitored and reported, not assumed away.

  5. years bone

    Structure that grows where it is loaded open question

    Mineral can be grown onto a steel frame by weak current — the method is fifty years old and is used to grow coral bases. Nobody has grown it where the structure itself reports the load. The segment under sustained strain receives the most current; a segment with no feedback grows at a fixed rate as the control.

    BuiltOne frame element, four segments, at least two years — because the honest objection to this idea is that it is slow
    MeasuredLayer thickness, sustained strain, segment stiffness, charge passed, reinforcement potential
    SuccessGrowth rate · energy per millimetre · growth in the loaded segment against the control · stiffness gained per millimetre

    Growth is far too slow to help during an earthquake, and this model does not claim otherwise. What it can do is come after: the network maps the damage at once, then thickens over months the places that took the blow. A bone does not survive the fall by growing; it grows because it fell.

  6. the loop one network

    One network that both senses and answers open question

    Each part above exists somewhere already, and each belongs to a different network with a different owner. The claim of this model is that one network does all of it: the same reinforcement that reports a crack carries the current that closes it. Closing that loop is the part nobody has built.

    BuiltOne conductive network, time-shared between sensing, heating and repair; a written rule that no action may drive the reinforcement anodic
    MeasuredHealth of the network itself; every action with the trigger that caused it; reinforcement potential; downtime
    SuccessCost against three separate systems · time from detection to action · functions lost per network failure · steel lost to stray current per year

    The obvious argument against a shared network is that one failure takes out three functions at once. That argument is not answered by enthusiasm. It is answered by the shared-failure number above, and if it loses, this page will say so.

  7. between
    houses     inheritance

    What the house inherits from other houses measured

    A house that has already lived a winter can hand its heating policy to a new house. We measured whether it helps to choose which house it comes from. It does not.

    Tested on40 source houses, 200 new ones, 19 measured climates, the full 8 000-pair matrix
    CrisisHalf the heating capacity lost for 72 hours, in the coldest window of each house's own weather record
    AnswerTake a policy. Do not spend effort choosing whose. Test candidates on a population and keep the winner

    This is the one system on this page with a result behind it rather than a plan — and the result is numerical, not built. No instance of this model exists yet.

Already measured

Choosing which house to learn from is worth one per cent

Across 8 000 ordered pairs, neither distance in ten building parameters nor a purpose-built resilience trait predicts the value of a transfer. The reason is that sources agree with each other: a source is good or bad for every house, not for some. Of the benefit a transfer brings, two thirds is which house received it and a quarter is which house produced it — leaving almost nothing for the pairing that everyone looks for.

What taking this model actually involves

A model is a contract in both directions. This is the side the client carries, and the side the library carries.

What you put in

  • The conditions written down before the first season, not after. Climate, size, use, occupancy. Experience without conditions is worse than no experience: a schedule that saved heat in one place can freeze a house in another.
  • The sensors from the systems you choose to build. The cheapest honest entry is one wall and five points, not a whole house.
  • One year of recording. Less than a year has no winter in it.
  • Two lines at the end: what happened, and what surprised you.

What you get back

  • A starting policy that already survived someone else's crisis, and the measured answer that you should not waste effort choosing whose.
  • Every later version of this model, with a written list of what changed against the version you built — so you can decide whether to follow.
  • The failures. A library that keeps only successes teaches nothing, so dead branches stay in it, marked dead.

The field that matters most is surprise. Expectations that were met teach nobody anything.
