Morphogenesis of a Living Building

A building that inherits not a finished body, but the capacity to form, maintain and restore it. Walk through the five steps: heredity, formation, twins, recovery and the inheritance cycle.

Step 1 · Section 3 of the paper

What exists before the building exists

Cloud DNA is a layered hereditary store. It holds rules, ranges and relationships, not a blueprint. The egg is the minimal hereditary entity from which one individual begins. Select a level.

Step 2 · Sections 4 and 6.1

    
From egg to body: growth from centres

    
Formation starts from one or more morphogenetic centres. At each step the rules from the egg choose where the next element is added: stay compact, turn toward the sun, avoid the windward side, and never build on rock. The body is recorded as a graph G(t) = (V(t), E(t)), where elements are nodes and typed relations are edges.

Step 4 · Sections 6.4 – 6.6 and 7

    
Regeneration as local re-activation of morphogenesis

    
Choose a damage. Each surviving centre is a candidate start for recovery. A centre is admissible only if it survived and can reach every required position, because equipment reach is limited. For each admissible pair (centre, trajectory) a declared cost J is computed, and the lowest one is starred. Click a row to replay that recovery. The same damage repaired from different centres gives different bodies.

Step 5 · Section 3

    
The hereditary cycle

    
The system does not copy bodies. It selects mechanisms that proved useful and returns them to Cloud DNA, where they become part of future eggs. Click any stage.

    

      

      

        
Cloud vs. local
The cloud preserves population-level heredity. The local seed inside the building preserves the developmental continuity of the individual, even if the link to the cloud is lost.

    

    

  


  
  

    
Section 6 of the paper

    
The preliminary mathematical representation

    
(1)B(t) = ( G(t), (si(t))i∈V(t), (re(t))e∈E(t) )The body at time t: the graph plus the state of every element and every relation. Step 2 draws G(t).

    
(2)Ti(D) ⊆ Ωi(D) × Ωi(D)Transitions between element states that the DNA D permits. Ωi(D) is the range of states of element i.

    
(3)Fi(B,η,R;D) ⊆ { s′ ∈ Ωi(D) : (si, s′) ∈ Ti(D) }States actually reachable now, given the body B, the environment η and the available resources R.

    
(4)A = { (c, π) : c ∈ C(t₀), π ∈ Pc(B₀, δ; D) }All admissible pairs of a recovery centre c and a trajectory π for task δ. Step 4 lists them in the table.

    
(5)(c*, π*) ∈ arg min(c,π)∈A J(c, π; δ)The selected pair is the one with the lowest declared cost. Step 4 marks it with ★.
