The exchange

Three sides, and each one has what the others lack

You have a calculation

A model for heat, airflow, structural fatigue, occupant comfort. It works in simulation. What you cannot get is a hundred real buildings running it for a year. Put the method here and the operational feedback comes back to you.

You have a building

An apartment block, an office, a workshop, a clinic. You pay for heat and water and repairs without knowing which part is waste. Take a method, enter your conditions, get a recommendation. The cost is a few sensors and honest reporting.

You want to build a business

Every field here is a separate business with its own customer: sensor installation, audits, retrofit design, monitoring subscriptions. The registry gives you the method and the accumulated evidence that it works.

What nobody here does

No one sells certainty. A method with two instances behind it is labelled as such. Results that came out worse than predicted are published exactly like the ones that came out better.

How it works

Four fields. Miss one and the record is worthless.

1 · Conditions

Climate, size, use, occupancy, what is already installed. Recorded before anything is done. A result without its conditions cannot be transferred anywhere — what saves heat in one climate can damage a building in another.

2 · Prediction

The method says what will happen, with a number, in writing, before the work starts. This is the field everyone skips and it is the one that matters: a prediction that cannot be wrong teaches nothing.

3 · Action

What was actually done — including the parts done differently from the recommendation. Those deviations are not noise. They are where better versions of a method come from.

4 · Outcome

Measured, not estimated. Then the only line that carries information: the gap between prediction and outcome. The method said eighteen per cent and reality gave eleven — those seven points are the whole lesson.

      How this grows

      Each stage is only possible because the previous one was done honestly

      00

      One method, one building

      Prove the loop closes at all: prediction written down, work done, outcome measured, record filed.

      01

      One method, many buildings

      Spread becomes visible. It becomes possible to say where the method is reliable and where it fails.

      02

      Versions appear

      A method is corrected, and the correction is tagged with the outcomes that caused it. Nothing changes without a reason on record.

      03

      Conditions start to matter

      With enough buildings in different conditions, the real question becomes answerable: when does one building's experience help another, and when does it hurt.

      04

      A new building starts ahead

      It receives not the first version of a method but the one that performed best under conditions like its own.

      Where this goes

      Stated plainly, so nobody has to guess

      The long-term question behind this registry is whether a building can keep what it learns during its service life and pass it to the next one — and at what distance between two buildings that transfer stops paying for itself and starts causing harm.

      That question is not answered by argument. It needs records from real buildings in different conditions, with predictions written down in advance. Everything on this site is the groundwork for it.

      Nothing here requires believing that a building is alive, and nothing here depends on it. Every problem listed above stands on its own and pays for itself on its own.
